Stromae's music skewers stereotypes. It troubles the stories we tell, questions how we cast ourselves - and others - in binary roles (even as we rail against them) and draws our contradictions into the light.

Even Stromae's name refuses to be still.

First it bends, with a verlan twist, into the figure of a Maestro. Then transforms again, slipping into the creative house of Mosaert, to echo another musical genius.

La musique de Stromae pourfend les stéréotypes. Elle interroge nos récits, expose comment on s'enferme soi - et les autres - dans des rôles binaires (même en les rejetant), et fait affleurer nos contradictions.

Même son nom refuse de tenir en place.

Stromae naît, par le verlan, de Maestro. Puis il se transforme encore, se glissant dans la maison créative Mosaert, faisant echo d'un autre génie musical.

Tous Les Mêmes tells the story of a relationship that is collapsing under the corrosive influence of misogyny & reductive gender roles. In the music video, Stromae embodies both partners, splitting himself in two to reveal their opposing views.

Bâtard examines the role of stereotypes more broadly, dissecting people into binaries to expose how categorising each other can prevent us from seeing, accepting, & embracing our differences.

T'es d'droite ou t'es d'gauche ? T'es beauf ou bobo d'Paris? | Sois t'es l'un ou soit t'es l'autre, t'es un homme ou bien tu péris | Cultrice ou patéticienne, féministe ou la ferme | mais t'es phobe ou sexuel?

Papaoutai & Quand c'est? set up a kind of dissonance, even an argument, between music and words: puncturing playful, upbeat melodies with lyrics that speak of abandonment, sickness, & death.

Or is it the reverse? Does the music bring light into the stories the lyrics recount, whilst also pushing us towards dance (& each other) as a counterpoint to pain?

Racine Carrée hints at a mathematical exercise: tracing something back to its root(s) & but also transforming the origin — through multiple iterations — into something new. What emerges might be very different from how it began, but it cannot be severed from its past, or its component parts.

This mirrors the album's explorations of heritage & hybridity, expressed through (harmonious) collisions of culture & sound. Through West African rhythms, Belgian EDM, hip-hop, Congolese rumba, chanson française, and more, Stromae creates music that hold stories for everyone - to be translated differently with every dance.
